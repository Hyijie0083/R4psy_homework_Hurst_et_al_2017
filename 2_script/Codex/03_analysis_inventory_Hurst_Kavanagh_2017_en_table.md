# Hurst & Kavanagh (2017) Analysis Inventory

Prepared from `01_analysis_inventory_Hurst_Kavanagh_2017.md` as an English, presentation-ready table.

| No. | Focus | Method | Key result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sample screening and participant profile | Descriptive statistics | 209 started, 71 excluded, final N = 138; 46 men and 92 women. |
| 2 | Scale reliability and descriptive statistics (Table 1) | Means, SDs, Cronbach's alpha | Core scales showed good to excellent internal consistency; e.g., Attachment alpha = .92, HKSS alpha = .91. |
| 3 | Convergence between life history indicators | Bivariate correlation | Mini-K and HKSS were strongly correlated (r = .71, p < .01). |
| 4 | Psychopathology threshold frequencies (Table 2) | Threshold counts and summed symptom index | DSM-5 item thresholds were tabulated; total psychopathology M = 40.10, SD = 14.57. |
| 5 | Gender-specific self-harm frequencies (Table 3) | Counts and percentages by behavior and sex | All 17 self-harm methods were reported separately for women and men. |
| 6 | Preliminary links of age and sex with outcomes (Table 4) | Correlation analysis | Older participants reported more secure attachment, less aggression, and fewer psychopathology symptoms; age was controlled later. |
| 7 | Life history strategy and family structure (Table 5) | Partial correlations controlling for age; Bonferroni-corrected | No association survived p < .002, though half-siblings and stepparent indicators showed trend-level links with faster strategies. |
| 8 | Life history strategy, psychopathology, and self-harm (Table 5) | Partial correlations controlling for age; Bonferroni-corrected | Faster strategies were linked to higher total psychopathology, but not to total self-harm frequency. |
| 9 | Does sex moderate life history strategy and self-harm? | Moderation analysis following Hayes (2013) | No main effect of sex and no sex x life history interaction. |
| 10 | Life history strategy and attachment (Table 5) | Partial correlations controlling for age; Bonferroni-corrected | Both Mini-K and HKSS were significantly related to all attachment indices; faster strategy mapped onto poorer attachment. |
| 11 | Does attachment independently predict life history strategy? (Table 6) | Two multiple linear regressions | Total attachment significantly predicted both Mini-K and HKSS after controlling for age and sibling counts. |
| 12 | Life history strategy and aggression (Table 5) | Partial correlations controlling for age; Bonferroni-corrected | Faster strategies were associated with more total aggression, physical aggression, anger, and hostility, but not verbal aggression. |
| 13 | Does sex moderate life history strategy and aggression? | Moderation analysis following Hayes (2013) | No moderating effect of sex; life history strategy remained the significant predictor. |
| 14 | Multiple-comparison control | Bonferroni correction | Main partial-correlation analyses used p < .002 (.05 / 30 = .001666). |

## Study overview

| Item | Summary |
|---|---|
| Design | Cross-sectional correlational study |
| Final sample | N = 138 (46 men, 92 women) |
| Main predictors | Mini-K and HKSS |
| Main outcomes | Attachment, aggression, psychopathology, self-harm, family structure |
| Frequent covariate | Age |
| Multiple-comparison rule | Bonferroni correction, p < .002 |
